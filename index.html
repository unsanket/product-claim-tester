Hypothesis Testing Step-by-Step Report

Product: Industrial LED Bulb • Claim: At least 1000

Step 1

Formulate Hypotheses

Establish the Null ($H_0$) and Alternative ($H_1$) hypotheses based on the claim:

Null Hypothesis ($H_0$): $\mu \ge 1000$ (Manufacturer claim)
Alternative ($H_1$): $\mu < 1000$ (Opposing claim / Defect)

Since $H_1$ points to values less than the claimed mean, this is a Left-Tailed (One-Tailed) Test.

Step 2

Significance Level

Determine the threshold for Type I error risk ($\alpha$):

$\alpha$: 0.05 (5.0%)
Confidence Level ($1 - \alpha$): 0.95 (95.0%)

There is a maximum 5% chance of incorrectly rejecting a true null hypothesis.

Step 3

Critical Value & Rejection Region

Determine the critical cutoff from standard normal $Z$-table:

Critical Value ($z_{\text{crit}}$): -1.645
Rejection Criterion: Reject $H_0$ if $z < -1.645$

If the calculated test statistic falls strictly inside the critical region, the claim fails the statistical test.

Step 4

Compute Test Statistic ($z$)

Calculate standard error and standardized distance from the mean:

$$z = \frac{\bar{x} - \mu_0}{\frac{\sigma}{\sqrt{n}}}$$
$$z = \frac{980 - 1000}{\frac{60}{\sqrt{36}}} = \frac{-20}{\frac{60}{6}} = \frac{-20}{10} = \mathbf{-2.0000}$$
Standard Error ($\text{SE}$) = $\frac{60}{6} = 10.0000$

The sample mean is 2.00 standard errors below the manufacturer's claimed mean.

Step 5

Compute P-Value

Exact probability of observing sample data at least as extreme:

$p$-value: 0.0228 (2.28%)
Comparison: $p\text{-value } (0.0228) < \alpha\ (0.05)$

Because $p < \alpha$, the empirical observation has less than 2.28% probability under $H_0$.

DECISION: REJECT H₀ (NULL HYPOTHESIS REJECTED)

The Product Fails the Claim Test

At the 5% level of significance, the calculated test statistic ($z = -2.0000$) falls in the critical rejection region ($z < -1.645$). Moreover, the $p$-value ($0.0228$) is less than $\alpha$ ($0.05$). Therefore, we reject the null hypothesis. There is statistically significant evidence that the true mean bulb life is less than 1,000 hours. The manufacturer's claim is refuted.

About Hypothesis Testing & Academic Scope

The 5 Core Steps of Hypothesis Testing

Hypothesis testing is an inferential statistical methodology used to assess whether sample evidence supports or refutes a specific hypothesis regarding a population parameter.

Step 1

State the Null ($H_0$) and Alternative ($H_1$) Hypotheses

The Null Hypothesis ($H_0$) represents the baseline claim or status quo (e.g. "The battery meets or exceeds 24h"). The Alternative Hypothesis ($H_1$) represents the assertion you want to verify (e.g. "The battery life is defective / under 24h").

Step 2

Select the Significance Level ($\alpha$)

The significance level ($\alpha$) specifies the maximum permissible risk of making a Type I error (rejecting a true null hypothesis). Typical standards in engineering are $\alpha = 0.05$ (5% risk, 95% confidence) or $\alpha = 0.01$ (1% risk, 99% confidence for mission-critical parts).

Step 3

Identify the Test Statistic and Critical Value ($z_{\text{crit}}$)

For large sample sizes ($n \ge 30$) or when population standard deviation ($\sigma$) is known, we use the standard normal $Z$-test. The critical value divides the normal distribution into the Acceptance Region and the Rejection Region.

Step 4

Compute the Test Statistic ($z$) from Sample Data

Calculate how many standard errors the sample mean $\bar{x}$ deviates from the hypothesized mean $\mu_0$: $$z = \frac{\bar{x} - \mu_0}{\frac{\sigma}{\sqrt{n}}}$$ where $\frac{\sigma}{\sqrt{n}}$ is the standard error of the mean ($\text{SE}$).

Step 5

Determine the $p$-Value and Formulate the Final Verdict

The $p$-value represents the probability of observing an outcome as extreme as the sample, assuming $H_0$ is true. If $p \le \alpha$ (or if $|z|$ falls in the critical region), we Reject $H_0$. Otherwise, we Fail to Reject $H_0$.

Key Terminology Reference

Symbol / Term Formal Name Engineering Definition Decision Rule / Significance
$H_0$ Null Hypothesis The hypothesis of "no difference" or the manufacturer's official specification claim. Assumed true until sample evidence strongly contradicts it.
$H_1$ (or $H_a$) Alternative Hypothesis The statement that contradicts $H_0$; represents an engineering flaw or claim dispute. Supported if $H_0$ is rejected.
$\alpha$ (Alpha) Significance Level Probability of rejecting $H_0$ when $H_0$ is actually true (Type I Error rate). Common values: 0.05, 0.01, 0.10.
$z_{\text{crit}}$ Critical Value Cutoff z-score corresponding to cumulative probability boundary of $\alpha$. Determines boundary of the critical rejection region.
$z$ Test Statistic Standardized distance between sample mean $\bar{x}$ and claimed mean $\mu_0$. Compared against $z_{\text{crit}}$ to determine statistical rejection.
$p$-value Probability Value Exact probability of observing sample evidence as or more extreme under $H_0$. If $p < \alpha$, Reject $H_0$. If $p \ge \alpha$, Fail to Reject $H_0$.
